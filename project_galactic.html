Galactic Rotation Curve: 21-cm Hydrogen Line Analysis

Unveiling the Dark Matter Mystery

Overview

This project investigated the kinematic structure of the Milky Way by capturing and analyzing 21-cm hydrogen line emissions. Utilizing a software-defined radio (RTL-SDR) pipeline, I processed raw astronomical data through spectral analysis and mathematical modeling—specifically the tangent point method—to derive galactic rotation velocities. The study combined hardware integration with computational data interpretation to empirically map the galaxy’s spiral geometry and validate theoretical rotation curves. In simple words, I used a software-defined radio to 'listen' to hydrogen gas in space and used that data to map out the shape and rotation speed of our galaxy.

Technical Process

Signal Acquisition – Captured 1420 MHz radio signals using hardware (antenna, LNA amplifier, SDR receiver) and converted to digital format.

Data Processing – Applied spectral analysis and mathematical transformations (tangent point method) to extract galactic distances and velocities.

Visualization – Generated rotation curve plots and compared results against reference models for validation.
