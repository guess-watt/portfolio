Galactic Rotation Mapping

Unveiling the Dark Matter Mystery

The Project

This project involves analyzing the rotation curve of the Milky Way galaxy using radio telescope data. By plotting the rotational velocity of stars and gas against their distance from the galactic center, we can observe discrepancies between predicted and observed velocities—a key piece of evidence for the existence of Dark Matter.

Methodology

Using the 21cm hydrogen line, we measured the Doppler shift of neutral hydrogen clouds in the galaxy. This data allows us to calculate the velocity of these clouds relative to us. By applying geometric models of the galaxy, these relative velocities are converted into orbital velocities at various radii.

Results

The resulting rotation curve remains flat at large distances from the center, rather than declining as expected from Keplerian dynamics based on visible mass alone. This "flat rotation curve" suggests that a significant amount of mass—Dark Matter—exists in the halo of our galaxy, providing the gravitational force needed to maintain these high velocities.
